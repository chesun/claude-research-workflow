## Verdict

The plan's core design is sound and well-adapted to the owner's actual situation: one profile at user scope is the right answer for an owner whose papers live in sibling repos (the user-scope default path is what lets `writer`/`writer-critic` dispatched from BDD or `bdm_bic` find the profile without any repo carrying it); the one-paper qualification rule (within-paper section recurrence, `evidence: within-paper`, reverting to the survey rule at 3 papers) is a defensible degradation honestly marked; the style-model separation (structure only, profile wins, never touches Lexicon/Quirks or catalog flags) is the right firewall; the BLOCKED corpus confirmation is honest and load-bearing; the carve-out and floor row IDs all exist in the catalog (`anti-ai-prose.md:70–123`), the cited line numbers (`writer.md:103`, `writer-critic.md:205`, `humanize/SKILL.md:90`, `docs/reference/skills.md:19`) all check out, and "no new hook, no gate" is consistent with gates-not-nags and ADR-0002. But the plan is weakest exactly where the question "does the profile actually work?" is decided: A4 is a single binary trial with a topic-shortcut confound, so a profile that captures the JMP's subject matter rather than the owner's voice would pass it; the acceptance evidence for A4/A5/A6 as specified would route owner prose into committed records (session log, before/after excerpts), undoing the privacy design that A2's one-time staged-diff grep cannot protect; and the claim that deliverable 5 "closes the Backlog item" is overstated, because `writer-critic.md:51` flags `implement` in its Cochrane list independently of the catalog and `TODO.md:43` names both critic files as needing edits the plan does not make. Two factual errors (CLAUDE.md and ADR-0006 are Class D excluded, not Class A synced; the pdftotext availability story does not hold on this machine) are correctable without touching the design. Fix the acceptance tests, the privacy-of-evidence rule, and the three factual claims, and the plan is fit for its purpose.
