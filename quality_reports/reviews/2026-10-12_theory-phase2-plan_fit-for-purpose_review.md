# Theory phase-2 plan Review — fit-for-purpose
**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Reviewer:** fit-for-purpose critic (independent, general-purpose agent)
**Target:** quality_reports/plans/2026-10-12_theory-workflow-phase2-lean-plan.md
**Score:** 58/100

**Lens:** does the plan achieve spec §4's purpose (catch misformalization; certify proofs) for an auditor who cannot read Lean? Read: the plan; spec §4 and acceptance #7–#8; ADR-0004; the phase-1 plan; `bin/blueprint_check.py` (hashing, log rules, report parsing); the `/theory` skill; survey synthesis and streams B and D; session log §6–§8; the pilot node `prop-nstar-interior.md` and its four dependency nodes in BDD; the spike project's `lakefile.toml` and `lean-toolchain`; the `.gitignore` of both repos; the MCP registration in `~/.claude.json`. Nothing was run or installed.

## Verdict

The plan is a faithful transcription of spec §4 and its mechanics for *certifying a proof* are sound: the `def <name>_stmt : Prop` split, the generated check file that re-types the proof against the audited `Prop`, `#print axioms` with the exact three-axiom set, `--wfail`, UNVERIFIED on a missing toolchain, and the validator's actor rule are all right, and the Review Focus list names the right adversarial cases. It is weak on the half of the purpose that matters for this owner: *catching misformalization*. The statement hash covers only the `_stmt` block, so the meaning of a signed statement can change through a helper definition or a `variable`/`open`/`notation` line without voiding the card; the card is drafted by the formalizer after it has already shaped the statement to be provable; the one independent channel (the blind back-translator) is fed the formalizer's own glossary; the sign-off is initials and a date with no verdict, no per-part map and no written answers; nothing tells the owner whether they can actually spot a bad card before they sign a real one; and the companions that are supposed to make the statement meaningful are never checked by `lean_check` at all. The pilot target is also the largest possible first card (a five-part conjunction whose part (d) cannot be faithfully stated by the interface the plan fixes). None of this is hard to fix, and most fixes add sections to the card and a stage to Task 6 rather than new machinery.

## If I were the owner

Three changes matter for someone who cannot read Lean, and they cost little:

1. **Sign before the proof, on a hash that covers the whole statement file** (findings 1 and 2). The thing you sign must be frozen, must include every definition that gives it meaning, and must not yet have been bent to fit a proof. Two-stage formalization and a whole-file hash give you that; everything downstream (card, report, validator) stays as planned.
2. **Make the independent channel truly independent and make yourself write something** (findings 3 and 10). The back-translator sees Lean only and writes its own glossary; you compare the two glossaries and fill in the per-part map and the concrete numeric instance by hand, then choose FAITHFUL / WITH-NOTED-DIFFERENCES / UNFAITHFUL. A card you had to fill in cannot be rubber-stamped.
3. **Calibrate yourself on toy cards and start with a small node** (findings 4 and 6). Read three short cards with a planted defect in one before the real one, and make the real one parts (a)–(c) of `prop:nstar-interior`, split up front. The first card is where you learn what a bad card looks like; it should not also be the hardest.

The proof-certification half (`_stmt`/proof split, check file, `#print axioms`, `--wfail`, UNVERIFIED on missing tools) is sound and should be kept as written, with the companions brought under the same `#print axioms` check (finding 5).
