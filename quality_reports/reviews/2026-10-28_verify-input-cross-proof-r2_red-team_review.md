# verify-input cross-proof refusal Review — red-team (round 2)
**Date:** 2026-10-28
**Reviewer:** red-team critic (independent, general-purpose agent)
**Target:** bin/blueprint_tools.py, bin/test_blueprint_tools.py
**Score:** 87/100
**Status:** Active
**Supersedes:** quality_reports/reviews/2026-10-28_verify-input-cross-proof_red-team_review.md

The redesign holds up where round 1 broke it. The ReDoS is dead: the round-1 timing script and 25 new ~100 KB pathological inputs (ambiguous item/separator runs, dotted numbers, paren-group chains, roman runs, possessive runs, "proofs of" tails, scan-view macro floods) all finish in under 90 ms, the item/separator grammar is genuinely non-overlapping, and the suite pins it (`xp_linear_on_adversarial_text`). Every round-1 WRONG is either fixed and re-verified (emphasis, `\cref`, brackets, unnumbered qualified steps, "argument/derivation", "Lemma lem:x", NBSP/`\,`/ZWSP-with-space) or pinned as an explicit decline with a corpus reason (the comma form, which the live corpus uses for a proof's own step; the pronoun, documented in the code comment and SKILL.md with item 11 as backstop) — I judge both declines sound. The live scan over 58 BDD nodes flags only two `refuted` variants (both genuine cross-proof pointers, neither eligible for verify-input), the base-kind skip correctly passes the live definition-step references, and the three 2026-10-15 proofs that motivated the change are each caught when run against their first git-history versions. What remains is the residue of a regex floor: a family of natural phrasings still walks through end-to-end into the sealed verifier input (possessive "lem:b's Step 2", bare plural "the steps of lem:b", ordinals past "third", "Step $2$"), and a small set of latent false positives ("step t in lem:b", statement-case "case 2 of lem:b") that have zero live instances today but would each block a real verification if the corpus's style drifts. None is corpus-attested the way round 1's were; all sit under the documented item-11 backstop. Ship it with the known-limits comment widened, and tighten the four cheap patterns below in a follow-up.
