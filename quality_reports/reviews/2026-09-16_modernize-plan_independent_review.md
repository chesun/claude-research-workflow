## Verdict (fit? efficient? bloated?)

**Fit for purpose: mostly yes; efficient: partly; bloated: in one direction, yes.** The plan's core diagnosis — keep fabrication/irreversibility *gates*, delete process *nags*, retire lab-scale propagation for a now-personal template — is sound, evidence-grounded, and correctly ordered to never leave live behavioral work broken. That half is a genuine simplification and should ship. But the plan carries an ironic bloat load for a "simplify" pass: its single biggest build (Phase 3: re-platform the whole orchestrator+critic loop onto a JS Workflow) plus the "add" items (Plugin packaging, method-spec check) are *new engineering* justified by "the primitives exist now," not by any pain the two personal behavioral projects actually feel. Two "simplify" moves also quietly *increase* fragility or scope: swapping the deterministic citation-detection regex for a model-call subagent inside a **blocking** PreToolUse hook, and retiring the evidence-ledger, which is wired far deeper (adversarial-default.md, no-assumptions.md, three critic agents, two reference docs) than the plan's one-line "SIMPLIFY" admits. Finally, the Phase-1 "no dangling references" gate rests on a hand-listed set of rules to edit that a `grep` proves is incomplete.

## What the plan gets right (keep this)

- **The gates-vs-nags organizing principle is correct and well-grounded.** Fabrication and irreversibility are the two failure modes a stronger model does not fix; everything else is process discipline Claude 5 handles. This is the right axis to cut on.
- **KEEP calls on the true gates are right:** destructive-action-guard + post-rewrite-verify (irreversibility; matches the OneDrive/shared-storage risk in this very repo path) and the primary-source citation gate (fabrication). Evidence-backed by the survey brief.
- **Pruning the four-hook compaction subsystem *together* is internally consistent.** context-monitor / session-reset / pre-compact / post-compact-restore are tightly coupled (session-reset only resets context-monitor's cache; post-compact-restore only reads the snapshot context-monitor/pre-compact write). You cannot half-prune them, and the plan prunes them as a unit — correct. Native automatic compaction plausibly subsumes the "where did I leave off" restore.
- **Pruning the process-nag Stop hooks** (log-reminder, output-length-check, plan-persist-check, diagnostic-claim-audit) is defensible; they are genuinely discipline nags, and their shared `stop_hooks_lib.py` has no other consumer, so cleanup is clean.
- **Incremental phasing that isolates the single live-project touch to a late, high-care phase** is the right risk posture.
- **Retiring propagation / overlay-sync / `/tools` lab subcommands given a dormant lab** is the highest-leverage real simplification in the whole plan.

## Sharpest single recommendation

**Split the plan in two and ship only the deletions now.** The real, low-risk simplify win is *removal*: prune the nag hooks (Phase 1) and retire the propagation/overlay machinery (Phase 6). Do those, and stop. Move Phase 3 (JS-Workflow re-platform), the subagent citation-detector, the ledger retirement, and Plugin packaging into a separate "modernize — optional, later" track, because each is net-new engineering that fights the plan's own simplify goal or deepens the scope far beyond its one-line label. And before executing Phase 1, replace the hand-written list of rules-to-edit with a mechanical `grep -rl <pruned-hook> .claude/` sweep — the current list is provably incomplete (misses derive-dont-guess.md, the adversarial-default.md ledger section, and workflow.md's context-management section), so "no dangling references" will not hold as written.
