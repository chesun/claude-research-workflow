**Date:** 2026-10-11
**Reviewer:** survey agent A (general-purpose, opus)
**Target:** Lean/Mathlib feasibility for economic theory proofs
**Status:** Active

Method: web search plus direct reads of Mathlib docs pages (leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs), Loogle name searches (loogle.lean-lang.org, indexed Mathlib revision `260040a` at time of query), GitHub READMEs, Reservoir package pages, arXiv papers and AFP entry pages. ## Bottom line

- **Area 1 (finite expected values, inequalities, IC arguments) is cheap and the best place to start.** Mathlib has finite sums, `PMF` with `PMF.integral_eq_sum`, finite Jensen (`ConvexOn.map_sum_le`), and conditional probability with Bayes (`ProbabilityTheory.cond_eq_inv_mul_cond_mul`). A BDM-style "truth-telling is weakly dominant" proof is a pointwise case split plus an expectation. EconCSLib has already done close cousins: Vickrey, reserve-price Vickrey, and a sorry-free Myerson lemma with integrals.
- **Area 2 (optimization and comparative statics) is mixed.** One-variable calculus is strong: Fermat (`IsLocalMax.hasDerivAt_eq_zero`), second-derivative test (`isLocalMin_of_deriv_deriv_pos`), and the convexity-from-derivatives lemmas. The implicit function theorem and equality-constrained Lagrange multipliers exist in Banach-space generality. Missing: KKT (an explicit Mathlib TODO), the envelope theorem/Danskin, Hessians and multivariate second-order conditions, and supermodularity/Topkis/single crossing (zero Loogle hits).
- **Comparative statics via the IFT is moderate-to-expensive in practice.** The Mathlib IFT is stated for `HasStrictFDerivAt` maps between Banach spaces. The one-dimensional `f : 𝕜 × 𝕜 → 𝕜` corollary with the explicit `-f_x/f_y` formula is itself listed as a TODO. Expect to write a reusable wrapper once.
- **Monotone comparative statics (Topkis, Milgrom–Shannon) is absent but elementary.** For finite or real-interval choice sets the proofs are short order-theoretic arguments, so formalizing them from scratch is cheap-to-moderate. This is a gap worth filling yourself.
- **Area 3 (probability and dynamics) is measure-theoretically deep but has economist-unfriendly API.** Mathlib has `MeasureTheory.condExp` (notation `μ[f|m]`), Markov kernels, `condKernel` disintegration, `ProbabilityTheory.posterior` (Bayes for kernels, notation `κ†μ`), Ionescu-Tulcea (`Kernel.traj`), invariant measures (`Kernel.Invariant`), and Banach's fixed point (`ContractingWith.fixedPoint`). It has no `MarkovChain` structure, no MDP or Bellman library and no Blackwell sufficient conditions. A finite-state discounted Bellman/contraction lemma is moderate. General-state Bayesian or dynamic-programming results are expensive.
- **Equilibrium existence:** Brouwer and Kakutani are *not* in Mathlib. Brouwer on simplices and mixed Nash existence are proved outside Mathlib (math-xmum/Brouwer, Lean/Mathlib v4.33.0, updated 2026-09-20). Kakutani is still an open entry on the Lean formalization leaderboard.
- **Prior econ work in Lean 4 is recent (mostly 2025–2026) and active.** It includes EconCSLib (40k+ lines, Lean v4.30.0), elazarg/GameTheory (1,363 commits), the Brouwer/Nash work, vNM and decision-theory projects, and Optlib (optimization, but on an old toolchain). Isabelle has the older and more mature econ corpus: Arrow/Gibbard–Satterthwaite, VCG, randomised social choice, and MDPs with Bellman optimality.
- **Recommended first targets:** finite-support IC/BDM lemmas, finite-type Bayesian updating, one-dimensional FOC plus concavity results, and a home-built supermodularity/Topkis module on finite lattices or intervals. Defer general-measure Bayesian updating, infinite-horizon DP on general state spaces, and multivariate KKT/envelope results until the cheap layer is in place.

## Feasibility verdicts

| Area | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Elementary/algebraic, finite expectations, IC (BDM, second-price style) | **High feasibility, cheap** | Proofs reduce to case splits and finite sums. EconCSLib already proves Vickrey/Myerson-type IC. Statement audit is easy because definitions are short. |
| (2a) 1-D FOCs, concavity, SOCs | **High, cheap** | Fermat, second-derivative test and deriv-convexity lemmas are all in Mathlib. |

| (2d) Lagrange (equality) | **Medium, moderate** | In Mathlib in Fritz-John form; the economist's regular-point form needs a short bridge. |
| (2e) KKT | **Low-medium, moderate–expensive** | Not in Mathlib. Optlib has it, but on a stale toolchain. |
| (2f) Topkis / supermodularity / single crossing | **High for finite or chain settings, cheap–moderate** | Absent, but mathematically elementary. A home-built module is a good investment. |
| (3a) Finite-state Bayesian updating | **High, cheap** | `cond`, Bayes and total probability lemmas exist. |

## Recommendations (what to formalize first)

1. **Start with finite-support IC lemmas.** Examples: BDM truthful reporting is weakly dominant for every price distribution, and second-price dominance. Types: `Fin n → ℝ` probability weights or `PMF` on a `Fintype`. Mirror the EconCSLib `MechBasic`/`Vickrey` definitions so later work can reuse them. These are cheap, and the theorem statements are short enough for the economist to audit line by line.
2. **Next, one-dimensional optimization lemmas.** Use FOC plus strict concavity to get a unique maximizer (`StrictConcaveOn`, `IsLocalMax.hasDerivAt_eq_zero`, `concaveOn_of_deriv2_nonpos`). Then add one reusable 1-D implicit-function / comparative-statics wrapper of the form `x*'(θ) = -f_θ/f_x`, built on `HasStrictFDerivAt.implicitFunction`.
3. **Build a small supermodularity / single-crossing module.** Cover increasing differences on chains, Topkis on finite or interval choice sets, and Milgrom–Shannon single crossing. Mathlib has nothing here, the proofs are elementary, and it pays off across behavioral and applied-micro theory.
4. **Finite-state Bayesian updating** with `ProbabilityTheory.cond` and `cond_eq_inv_mul_cond_mul`. Use real-valued wrappers to avoid `ℝ≥0∞` friction.
5. **Defer** general-distribution posterior arguments, KKT with inequality constraints, the general envelope theorem, and infinite-horizon DP on general state spaces. If DP is essential, do a finite-state discounted Bellman contraction first, following the Isabelle MDP-Rewards structure, and only then generalize.
6. **Audit discipline (from EconCSLib's own lessons).** AI is strong at routine proof engineering but can produce statements that "do not capture the intended mathematical object". The economist should therefore check definitions (mechanism, IC, type space, measure assumptions) before trusting any proof. A `#print axioms` / "kernel-clean" check, as in the WDK paper, guards against hidden `sorry` or axioms. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16144v1 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08902
